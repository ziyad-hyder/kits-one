    FAQ

    Common questions about SGPA, Bunk Buffer, ESE targets, and regulations

    How is SGPA calculated at KITSW, and why are F-grade credits excluded?
    GP

    Grade point scale

    • S = 10  ·  A = 9  ·  B = 8  ·  C = 7  ·  D = 6  ·  P = 5  ·  F = 0
    ÷

    SGPA = sum(Credits × Grade Point) ÷ sum(Cleared Credits)

    F = 0 GP, so an F subject drops out of both the numerator and denominator. It doesn't drag your GPA down — it just doesn't count at all, per URR-24 rule 12.11.

    eg

    Worked example

    • 3-credit A (9) + 3-credit B (8) + 3-credit F (0)
    • SGPA = (3×9 + 3×8) / (3+3) = 8.50
    • Cleared Credits: 6 / 9 — the F just sits there, embarrassed.
    How many classes can I bunk and stay above 75%?
    ≥0

    If you're above 75%

    Bunks available = floor(0.25 × Held − Absent)
    100 held, 18 absent → floor(25 − 18) = 7 bunks left.

    <0

    If you've already slipped below 75%

    Classes needed = ceil(Absent ÷ 0.25 − Held)
    100 held, 30 absent → ceil(120 − 100) = 20 straight classes to recover.

    Tip: Don't type every subject by hand. Paste your UMS Attendance Report into the autofill box — it fills Held & Absent for every subject automatically.
    How do I convert CGPA to percentage and to US 4.0 for MS applications?
    %

    Percentage (URR-24 rule 17.4a)

    (CGPA − 0.50) × 10  →  8.50 CGPA = 80.0%

    4.0

    US 4.0 Scale (for MS abroad applications)

    (CGPA ÷ 10) × 4  →  8.50 CGPA = 3.40 / 4.0

    Note: These are the official KITSW formulas. Some foreign universities use their own conversion tables — check with the institution before you apply.
    How does the ESE planner work, and what do I need in the endsem for a target grade?
    1

    Enter your CIE marks for each subject

    • URR24-R25 theory: CIE Total (out of 150)
    • URR24-R25 lab: CIE + Lab Internal (60) + Lab External (40)
    • URR26 theory: MSE-I, MSE-II, Teacher's Assessment — weighted as 0.7 × best + 0.3 × other + TA
    2

    Pick a target grade for each subject

    • S = 90% · A = 80% · B = 70% · C = 60% · D = 50% · P = 40%
    • It tells you exactly how many marks you need in the ESE.
    Stop the blind panic before externals. If the required ESE score is higher than the max (100 for theory, 60 for labs), it flags that grade as not possible — lower the target, not your self-worth.
    What is the difference between URR24-R25 and URR26, and how do I switch?

    Two regulations, two completely different marking schemes:

    • URR24-R25 — Theory: 150 CIE + 100 ESE (250 total). Labs: 150 CIE + 60 Lab Int + 40 Lab Ext + 100 ESE (350 total).
    • URR26 — All subjects: 40 CIE + 60 ESE (100 total). Theory CIE is weighted across MSE-I, MSE-II, and TA.

    To switch: Use the regulation dropdown in the top-right header, right next to the dark mode toggle. Your choice saves locally — the subjects, credits, and ESE rules all reload automatically.

    Not sure which one? If you joined KITSW in 2024 or 2025, you're on URR24-R25. 2026 batch is URR26.
